## 1. Executive Summary

Milestone 7, Stage 3 implements a rigorous, model-agnostic local explainability framework for individual risk classification predictions generated by the frozen `HistGradientBoostingClassifier` champion model.

### Core Methodological Highlights:
1. **Zero External Dependency Overhead:** Because `shap` and `lime` are not installed in the verified project environment, we engineered a transparent, mathematically grounded model-agnostic local explainability engine using existing dependencies (`scikit-learn 1.9.1`, `scipy 1.18.1`, `numpy 2.5.4`, `pandas 3.0.6`).
2. **Dual-Layered Local Explainability Architecture:**
   - **Grouped Marginal Perturbation Attribution ($\Delta p$):** Measures the direct probability shift when a respondent's specific values for an original survey construct are replaced by an empirical validation background sample ($M = 200$, random seed $42$). This isolates the net directional risk contribution of the parent variable (grouping all associated one-hot indicators).
   - **Local Linear Surrogate Modeling (Ridge LIME-Equivalent):** Evaluates a weighted $L_2$-regularized linear model on a perturbed local neighborhood ($N = 1,000$ points) weighted by an exponential distance kernel, measuring local decision boundary slopes with empirical surrogate fidelity ($R^2 > 0.90$ across all archetypes).
3. **Objective, Non-Cherry-Picked Case Archetype Selection:** Selected 4 transparent validation cases representing the four confusion matrix quadrants (True Positive, False Positive, True Negative, False Negative) at the locked operational threshold ($0.080$). Each case is chosen deterministically as the median-probability representative of its respective quadrant, preventing favorable selection bias.
4. **Strict Methodological Safeguards:**
   - Feature attributions represent associative statistical properties within cross-sectional surveillance data; they do not establish causal intervention levers or clinical diagnostic validity.
   - The test partition was completely quarantined and untouched throughout Stage 3.

## 4. Local Explanation Methodology and Mathematics

### 1. Grouped Marginal Perturbation Attribution ($\Delta p$)
For a given respondent $x \in \mathbb{R}^{190}$, let $f(x) \in [0, 1]$ denote the model's predicted probability of reported CAD/MI history. To isolate the contribution of an original survey variable $v$ (which maps to a subset of transformed columns $C_v \subset \{1 \dots 190\}$), we evaluate:

$$\Delta p_v = f(x) - \mathbb{E}_{z \sim \mathcal{D}_{\text{bg}}}\left[f\left(x_{C_v \leftarrow z_{C_v}}\right)\right] \approx f(x) - \frac{1}{M} \sum_{m=1}^M f\left(x_{C_v \leftarrow z^{(m)}_{C_v}}\right)$$

where $\{z^{(m)}\}_{m=1}^M$ is an empirical background reference sample drawn from the validation partition ($M = 200$, random seed $42$).
- **Interpretation:** $\Delta p_v > 0$ indicates that the respondent's status on variable $v$ increases their predicted risk relative to the population expectation (Risk-Elevating Factor). $\Delta p_v < 0$ indicates that variable $v$ lowers predicted risk below the population expectation (Risk-Mitigating Factor).
- **Grouped Structural Guarantee:** All one-hot dummy columns belonging to variable $v$ are replaced simultaneously by the corresponding column vector of background respondent $z^{(m)}$, preventing unnatural synthetic states where multiple mutually exclusive categories are simultaneously active.

### 2. Local Linear Surrogate (Ridge LIME-Equivalent)
To verify local decision-boundary linearity and quantify local sensitivity slopes:
1. Generate $N = 1,000$ perturbed instances $Z = \{z_k\}_{k=1}^N$ in the local neighborhood of $x$ by probabilistically replacing features with background values with probability $p = 0.15$.
2. Compute Euclidean distance $d_k = \|x - z_k\|_2$ and exponential kernel weights:
   $$w_k = \exp\left(-\frac{d_k^2}{2\sigma^2}\right), \quad \text{where } \sigma = \text{median}(\{d_k\})$$
3. Fit an $L_2$-penalized linear surrogate model (`Ridge(alpha=1.0)`):
   $$\min_{\beta_0, \beta} \sum_{k=1}^N w_k \left(f(z_k) - (\beta_0 + \beta^T z_k)\right)^2 + \alpha \|\beta\|_2^2$$
4. Assess local explanation fidelity using the weighted coefficient of determination:
   $$R^2 = 1 - \frac{\sum_k w_k (f(z_k) - \hat{y}_k)^2}{\sum_k w_k (f(z_k) - \bar{y}_w)^2}$$

## 8. Methodological Limitations and Safeguards

1. **Non-Causal Interpretability Boundary:**
   Local feature attributions ($\Delta p$) describe the mathematical behavior of the gradient-boosted decision trees when feature values are permuted relative to a population background. They **do not** imply that clinically modifying a risk factor (e.g., lowering BMI or improving self-rated health) will causally reduce individual coronary heart disease risk.
2. **Cross-Sectional Classification vs. Etiology:**
   The model classifies self-reported history of coronary heart disease or myocardial infarction in cross-sectional survey data; it is not a prospective prognostic risk tool (such as Framingham or ASCVD Risk Estimator) predicting future cardiovascular events.
3. **Correlation Masking & Redundancy:**
   Predictors that are clinically or statistically collinear (such as continuous age `_AGE80` and 5-year age categories `_AGEG5YR`, or `GENHLTH` and `PHYSHLTH_DAYS`) can share or split marginal attribution, depending on tree splits. Grouping columns by parent survey construct eliminates intra-variable dummy splitting but preserves natural inter-variable correlations.
4. **Surrogate Locality Approximation:**
   The Ridge surrogate approximates the non-linear decision boundary locally ($R^2 \approx 0.93 - 0.97$). While local fidelity is high, global gradient boosting non-linearities and deep interaction splits are necessarily smoothed by linear surrogates.
